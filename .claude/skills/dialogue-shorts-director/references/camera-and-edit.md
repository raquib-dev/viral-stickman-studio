# Camera and edit

## Measured edit rhythm (all seven reels)

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Shots per reel | 18-26 |
| Average shot length | 1.79-2.56 s, typically about 2.1 s |
| First spoken word | 0.37-0.41 s, in every reel |
| Gap between speech segments | median 0.47 s, 10th-90th percentile 0.15-0.87 s |
| Share of gaps | under 0.3 s: 22 %; 0.3-0.6 s: 57 %; 0.6-1.0 s: 17 %; over 1 s: 4 % |
| Talking time | 57-72 % of the run from first to last word |
| Cut vs. the next speech start | median 0.15 s before, 10th-90th percentile 0.20-0.13 s before |
| Cut vs. the previous line's end | median 0.33 s after |
| Last word to the end of file | about 1.6-1.7 s, the pull-back |

**What that means in practice:**
- The picture changes to the next speaker about 4-5 frames before they speak. The viewer sees the
  face, then hears the voice.
- The pause between lines is about half a second. That is a natural turn and not dead air.
- A beat (0.6-1.0 s) is rare and earned. It usually comes before a "..." line.
- Quick answers (0.15-0.3 s) are about one in five, and they are mostly a speaker continuing their
  own sentence, or a sharp comeback.

## The shot vocabulary

1. **Her MCU (medium close-up), the workhorse.**
   - Eye level, frontal or a slight 3/4 angle.
   - Head and shoulders, with her head filling about 45-55 % of the frame width and the top of her
     hair near the top edge.
   - The window and skyline behind her are softly blurred.
   - She is centred most often. About a third of the time she is set left or right of centre.
   - A variant looks down at a phone from a slightly higher camera [v2, v6].
2. **The mascot at desk height, the second workhorse.**
   - The camera sits at desk level, a short distance from the mascot.
   - The mascot is sharp, takes about 45-60 % of the frame width, and sits in the lower half.
   - Behind it, the girl's yellow torso and arm fill the background, heavily blurred. Her face is
     usually cut off at the top or out of focus.
   - **Alternate sides:** her body behind the mascot's right on one shot and its left on the next.
     The mascot moves to the opposite third.
   - In group episodes each AI gets the same treatment from its own place on the desk.
3. **The frontal wide.**
   - The camera is slightly above the desk and centred on her.
   - The whole desk is in frame: her at the centre, the mascots in front, the monitor at the right,
     the window and skyline above.
   - Use it for:
     - the opening line when it introduces a group or a new character [v4, v5];
     - one or two mid-story lines, often the ones that land the premise or the twist;
     - the final line.
4. **The whiteboard variant [v3]:**
   - a 3/4 wide from behind her right shoulder that shows the board, Claude on its books with the
     pointer, and her in profile;
   - her MCU at 3/4 in profile, glancing at the board;
   - Claude on the books, close.
   - The board shot carries every explanation line, so the graphics can be read.

## Movement
- Every shot moves a little: a slow push-in of about 2-4 % over the shot, with no handheld shake.
  Nothing is perfectly static.
- The characters don't move inside a shot beyond blinks, mouths and eyes. Poses change on cuts.
- **The ending:** the last line plays on the frontal wide. When the talking ends the camera keeps
  dollying back slowly, about 1.5-2 s at an even pace, revealing more desk and the lamp. There is no
  fade to black, the cut ends on the move, and the bed tapers out.

## Cutting rules
- Hard cuts only. No dissolves, wipes or zooms between shots.
- One shot per line. Hold the shot when the same person says two lines in a row, or cut to the same
  person in a different framing.
- The cut lands about 0.15 s before the next speaker starts. To show a reaction, the cut can land in
  the gap about 0.3-0.6 s after the line ends, onto the listener, before they answer.
- Don't repeat the same framing twice in a row. Alternate her MCU with the mascot's desk-height
  shot, and drop in the wide about every 8-12 lines.
- An interrupted line is cut on the interruption: the new speaker's shot comes in where the caption
  stops mid-sentence [v4 "Should it have a"].
